PAIN

Project demand does not always arrive evenly.

When several projects release at once, internal cutting and bending
can become a bottleneck just as engineering changes are still
occurring.

Potential consequences include:

- Delayed assembly
- Delayed testing
- Delayed installation
- Risk to customer commitments
- Loss of control over released revisions
- Obsolete-file risk
- Rework
- Additional supplier questions
- Fabricated parts becoming a project critical-path constraint

Approved central pain statement:

When several projects release at once, internal fabrication capacity
can fall behind just as designs are still changing. That puts
assembly, testing, installation, and customer commitments at risk.

CLAIM

Graymills helps project-based manufacturers add or absorb selected
fabrication capacity while maintaining control of released designs
and protecting downstream schedules.

Approved claim statement:

Graymills helps project-based manufacturers absorb cut-and-bend
demand while maintaining control of released designs and protecting
downstream schedules.

The claim must describe the customer improvement. It must not merely
list Graymills equipment, software, or capabilities.

GAIN / PROOF

Current process-based proof supported by the source includes:

- Graymills is an established industrial-equipment manufacturer,
  not only a fabrication job shop.

- Graymills understands how fabricated parts affect welding,
  assembly, serviceability, and completion of finished equipment.

- SolidWorks PDM supports controlled drawings, revisions, and
  released files.

- Work can begin with a defined cut-and-bend package rather than
  requiring a broad outsourcing commitment.

- Graymills is local to its target market, supporting practical
  communication and logistics.

- A relationship may later expand into welding, powder coating,
  and assembly when appropriate and separately confirmed.

Approved Gain / Proof statement:

Graymills supports the claimed outcome through SolidWorks PDM
revision control, practical industrial-equipment manufacturing
experience, defined fabrication packages, and a local production
relationship designed for close technical coordination.

CAMPAIGN THEME

When Project Demand Spikes, Keep Fabrication Off the Critical Path

This theme connects two supported ideas:

1. Project peaks can overload internal cutting and bending.
2. A relatively small fabricated package can delay a much larger
   project.

PERSONA ADAPTATION

Executive and C-suite:

Focus on growth, fixed-cost exposure, capital allocation, temporary
capacity requirements, utilization risk, and scaling without
overbuilding.

Approved claim direction:

Add scalable fabrication capacity without building permanent
capacity around peak demand.

Do not state a specific avoided capital amount unless a separately
approved source supports that number.

Operations and Manufacturing:

Focus on bottlenecks, overtime, downstream delays, schedule
instability, overflow, and production flow.

Approved claim direction:

Add project capacity while protecting assembly, testing, and
installation schedules.

Engineering:

Focus on revision control, released files, manufacturability,
supplier questions, rework, and assembly fit.

Approved claim direction:

Move released designs into fabrication without losing revision
discipline or creating more engineering work.

Project and Program Leadership:

Focus on critical-path risk, milestones, testing, installation,
customer acceptance, and schedule accountability.

Approved claim direction:

Keep fabricated parts from becoming the reason the project slips.

Practitioners:

Focus on correct files, clear communication, fewer questions,
less rework, reliable dates, easier repeat orders, and reduced
internal disruption.

Approved claim direction:

Get cut-and-bent parts produced from the correct released files,
with clear communication and fewer schedule disruptions.

MESSAGING RULES

1. Lead with the customer's operating problem, not Graymills'
   equipment.

2. State the customer outcome before listing capabilities.

3. Use SolidWorks PDM as proof of revision discipline, not as the
   claim itself.

4. Keep claims narrow, credible, and defensible.

5. Do not invent delivery, quality, productivity, savings, capacity,
   or financial results.

6. Use process-based proof until quantified customer evidence is
   available.

7. Match the message to the persona's level of responsibility.

8. Connect daily friction to operational consequences and business
   consequences.

9. Use one low-friction next step.

10. Keep initial outreach concise.

CLAIM BOUNDARIES

Do not claim or imply that Graymills has documented results for any
of the following unless a separate approved source is available:

- On-time delivery performance
- Quote turnaround time
- Revision-error rate
- First-pass acceptance rate
- Engineering response time
- Repeat-order performance
- Measured overtime reduction
- Measured backlog reduction
- Measured assembly-readiness improvement
- A specific amount of available fabrication capacity
- A specific avoided capital investment
- Guaranteed schedule improvement
- Guaranteed quality improvement
- Guaranteed productivity improvement

These are evidence areas to develop, not current proof.

Do not present welding, powder coating, or assembly as automatically
included in every project. The source supports only that a
relationship may later expand into those services where separately
confirmed.

AI APPLICATION RULES

When analyzing a company in the Job Shop Fab category:

- Use external company research and CRM evidence to determine
  whether project peaks, released-design control, internal
  fabrication constraints, or downstream schedule risk are
  relevant.

- Present those conditions as hypotheses until supported by company
  evidence.

- Do not state that the prospect has a bottleneck, overtime issue,
  revision problem, or late-project problem without evidence.

- Use the Pain–Claim–Gain sequence:
  Pain, then Claim, then Gain / Proof.

- Keep all recommendations within the assigned Job Shop Fab
  category.

- Prefer one defined fabrication package as a low-risk discovery
  starting point.

- Ask for discovery where evidence is missing rather than inventing
  operational conditions or results.
